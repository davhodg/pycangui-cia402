# Motor control (CiA 402)

A [plugin](https://github.com/davhodg/pycangui/blob/master/pycangui/help/plugins.md) for pycangui, kept in its own
[repository](https://github.com/davhodg/pycangui-cia402). *Plugins > Manage plugins...*,
*Check for plugins*, installs it.

It drives a motor controller: its state, its mode,
its target and what it is actually doing.

## Why it is a plugin

Half of that screen could have been a custom pane, and it is worth knowing
which half. The modes, the targets and the actual values are ordinary objects
at standard indices -- point a custom pane at 0x6060, 0x60FF and 0x606C and you
have them, with no code at all.

The other half cannot be. A drive does nothing until it has been walked
through a state machine -- 0x06, then 0x07, then 0x0F -- and *which* of those
writes is needed depends on what the drive answered to the last one. A fault
is cleared by a rising edge rather than by a value, so it is two writes. And
the state is not a value: it is decoded from overlapping masks of the
statusword, where "Ready to switch on" and "Switched on" differ in one bit
while "Fault" is a different mask altogether. No arrangement of boxes on a
form expresses any of that.

## The layout

The drive, its NMT state and the drive's **State** with *Enable*, *Disable*,
*Quick stop* and *Reset fault* are at the top, above two tabs, so stopping is
always in reach:

- **Run**: the mode and target, and what the drive is doing.
- **Settings**: what is set once for a machine rather than while it runs --
  limits, ramps, homing, position windows and how it stops -- read and
  written when asked. See [Settings](#settings).

## The node's NMT state

Beside the node is its **NMT state** -- Pre-operational, Operational or Stopped
-- as its heartbeat last gave it. That is the CANopen state, not the drive's:
SDO, which is all this pane writes, works in Pre-operational and Operational,
and PDOs only flow in Operational. A node that sends no heartbeat says *not
known (no heartbeat)*, since a heartbeat is the only way pycangui learns the
state, and a node whose heartbeat has stopped says *lost*. Nothing is sent to
find the state out.

**Pre-operational** and **Start** beside it send the selected node that NMT
command. *Start* asks first, once per node, as *Enable* does: once Operational,
an RPDO it is sent is acted on, and may move it.

If an enabled RPDO of the node carries something this pane writes -- the
controlword, the mode, or the target of the mode it is in -- a warning under
the node names it. While the node is Operational, whatever sends that RPDO
(CAN Transmit here, or another master) overrides what this pane writes, so a
write from here can appear to do nothing or flick back. It is only said: which
of the two should win is a decision about the machine. The mapping is the one
pycangui holds, from the EDS or from *Read PDO config* in the CANopen pane.

## Enabling

**Enable is designed to ask first**, once per drive per session, the same as joining a live
bus or transmitting onto one: it is the moment a motor becomes able to move,
and if a target is already set it may move immediately.

**Velocity mode (2)** has a target of its own, **vl target velocity**
(0x6042, 16-bit, in rpm), rather than 0x60FF, which is profile velocity's and
cyclic sync velocity's. It also needs controlword bits 4, 5 and 6 set --
enable the ramp, unlock it, use the reference -- or its ramp holds the drive
at zero whatever the target says. In velocity mode the pane sets those three
in every controlword it writes, and *Set target* on a drive already enabled
writes the enable controlword again with them, so a drive enabled before the
mode was chosen moves as well. In the other modes those bits mean other
things, and are left alone.

In every mode but profile position, *Set target* on a drive already enabled
writes the enable controlword after the target, without the **halt** bit. The
pane halts the drive itself whenever it is put away or paused (see
[Stopping](#stopping)), and a halted drive keeps its velocity demand (0x606B)
at zero whatever target it is given. A drive that is not enabled is left
alone: enabling is asked about first. If the demand still does not move, a
profile velocity drive with a profile acceleration (0x6083) of zero has no
ramp to climb. *What it is doing* shows velocity mode's own speed,
**vl velocity actual value** (0x6044), in rpm, in place of 0x606C.

## Settings

What is set once for a machine, on a tab of its own. It is read when the tab
is first shown for a drive, and again on *Read all*; nothing on it is polled.
A value typed or chosen is tinted until it is written, as on a custom pane,
and *Write changed* sends those and nothing else -- asking first, since a
higher limit, a faster ramp or a different way of stopping changes what the
machine does whatever is asking. An object the drive's EDS does not have is
greyed and not read; one the drive refuses is named, all together, in the
Event Log. A code a drive answers with that CiA 402 does not list -- a
maker's own, below zero -- is shown as its number.

- **Limits**: max torque (0x6072), max profile velocity (0x607F), max motor
  speed (0x6080), and the software position limits (0x607D).
- **Profile**: profile velocity (0x6081), acceleration (0x6083), deceleration
  (0x6084) and quick stop deceleration (0x6085).
- **Homing**: the method (0x6098), by what it homes on; the speed to the
  switch and the speed to zero (0x6099); the acceleration (0x609A); and the
  home offset (0x607C).
- **Position**: the following error window (0x6065), the position window
  (0x6067), and the polarity (0x607E), which reverses positions or
  velocities.
- **Stopping**: what the drive does on quick stop, halt, shutdown, disable
  operation and a fault (0x605A to 0x605E), in words. Quick stop and halt
  here are what *Quick stop*, and the pane closing (see
  [Stopping](#stopping)), make it do.
- **Scaling**, shown and not written: the units (0x60A8, 0x60A9), the
  encoder resolution (0x608F) and the velocity factor (0x6096) that the
  units and conversions on the pane come from.

### Limits

These are not targets: a drive is held to them whatever mode it is in and
whatever is asking.

The two speed limits are two different objects because CANopen measures
speed in two different ways. The velocity objects -- 0x606C, 0x60FF, 0x6081
and 0x607F -- are in the drive's own velocity units, whatever it was set up
in: increments per second, rpm, millimetres per second. Velocity mode has its
own objects, 0x6042 and 0x6044, in rpm by definition, and 0x6080 is about the
motor rather than about a profile, and is in rpm too. See [Units](#units).

So that they can be compared, each is also shown in the other's unit, greyed
beside it: 0x607F in rpm and 0x6080 in the drive's velocity units. That is
worked out from 0x60A9 where it names revolutions, radians or increments
against time; otherwise from the **position encoder resolution** (0x608F:
increments per motor revolutions) and, where the drive has one, the
**velocity factor** (0x6096), taking the velocity units as increments per
second as a drive without a factor group counts. Where the drive gives none
of that, or its velocity units are rpm already, nothing is shown.

## Only what this drive has

If the selected node has an [EDS](https://github.com/davhodg/pycangui/blob/master/pycangui/help/canopen.md) loaded, it says which objects
exist, and a control for one this drive does not have is switched off rather
than left to fail with an abort code nobody reads. Without an EDS everything
is available: the profile says these objects exist, most drives have them, and
greying out a control because pycangui was never given a file would be worse
than an SDO that comes back refused. An EDS loaded while the pane is open
counts straight away rather than at the next time the drive is picked, and an
object the file does not have is not polled for either.

The mode list is what 0x6502 reports, which an EDS cannot tell you -- every
drive's EDS lists 0x6060, and none of them says that this drive cannot do
torque. *No mode* is always available, since writing 0 is how a drive is told
to be in none of them. A drive that does not answer 0x6502 gets the full
list.

What it is designed to refuse is as much of the point as what it does. A drive still
starting up, or still reacting to a fault, is left alone -- it leaves those
states by itself, and a controlword written then is ignored rather than
refused, which looks exactly like the tool having done nothing. Quick stop is
available only to a drive that is running. A target is not available at all in the
cyclic synchronous modes, where it has to arrive every cycle over a PDO and one
written by hand would be stale before it got there. A value too big for its
object is refused rather than wrapped. And the button that tells a profile
position drive to take its target is available only while the drive is already
enabled, because making that happen means writing the enable controlword -- a
button that quietly does what another button asks permission for is a hole in
the permission.

**Go** is that button. In profile position, *Set target* only stores the
position; Go writes the enable controlword with bit 4 off, on and off again,
and the drive starts the move on the rising edge. Every other mode with a
target moves as soon as *Set target* writes it, so there Go is greyed, with
*(Profile position only)* beside it.

Under it, in profile position, two ticks go with Go's edge: **Relative**
(bit 6), the target a distance from where the drive is rather than a place,
and **Change immediately** (bit 5), the new target taken at once rather than
after the move under way.

In **homing** (mode 6) the button is **Start homing**: the enable controlword
with bit 4 off and then on, by the method, speeds and offset on the
[Settings](#settings) tab. Bit 4 is left on, since clearing it stops homing
part way; *Quick stop*, *Disable*, or the pane going (see
[Stopping](#stopping)) stops it. Only while the drive is enabled, as Go. Under
it, what homing is doing, from statusword bits 10, 12 and 13: in progress,
interrupted or not started, done, or an error, still moving or stopped.

## What it is doing

*Poll* reads the statusword, the mode in use (0x6061), position (0x6064),
velocity (0x606C, or 0x6044 in velocity mode) and torque (0x6077), and below
them the **demand** of the mode the drive is in: what its profile generator is
asking of the motor, before the motor has followed.

| Mode | Demand |
|---|---|
| Profile position, cyclic sync position | Position demand, 0x6062 |
| Velocity | vl velocity demand, 0x6043, in rpm |
| Profile velocity, cyclic sync velocity | Velocity demand, 0x606B |
| Profile torque, cyclic sync torque | Torque demand, 0x6074 |

Beside the actual value it says which of two things is wrong. Demand still at
zero with a target set: the drive has not taken the target -- halted, or no
acceleration to ramp with. Demand moving while the actual value is not: the
motor is not following. Homing has no demand to show.

**Inputs** (0x60FD) is read with them: which of the drive's limit switches,
home switch and interlock are active, and the maker's inputs (bits 16 to 31)
by number -- often the answer to why a drive will not move one way.

Beside the state, statusword bits 12 and 13 are named as the mode defines
them: set-point acknowledged and following error in profile position, speed
zero and max slippage in profile velocity, homing attained and homing error
in homing, and following the command in the cyclic modes.

When the drive goes to **Fault**, the pane reads its **error code** (0x603F)
once and shows it under the state, with what CiA 301 calls it. It goes when
the fault is reset, and a new fault is read again.

## Which object

Every value on the pane names the object behind it in its tooltip -- index,
sub-index, name and unit -- and *Poll* lists the ones it reads. A spin box
holding a number is not obviously 0x6072 rather than 0x6080, and that is the
first thing anybody comparing this screen with a drive manual wants to know.

## Manufacturer bits

Both words have bits the profile hands to the maker: 11 to 15 in the
controlword, 8, 14 and 15 in the statusword. What they mean is in the drive's
manual and nowhere else -- one drive's bit 15 is a brake release and another's
is a spindle orientation request -- so pycangui shows and sets them by number
and does not pretend to name them. Statusword bits 12 and 13 are *not* here:
those are defined per mode by the standard, and calling them manufacturer bits
would mislead anybody reading along with a manual. Every number on that row
is a bit position in decimal, never a value: the pane says *statusword bit
14* rather than *statusword: 14* for exactly that reason, and the statusword
itself is on the line directly above, in hex.

A ticked controlword bit is **held in every controlword this pane writes**,
the commands and the halt on the way out included. Some drives will not act on
anything unless one of these is set, so a bit dropped from the one write that
stops the machine would be worse than a bit that was never available. The ticks
are not remembered between sessions: a bit that releases a brake is not
something to restore quietly at startup.

## When the drive will not answer

An SDO that aborts is said in the Event Log, with the object it was for and
the code the drive gave. It used to be dropped in silence while polling: the
value on screen simply stopped changing, which reads as a drive holding steady
rather than as a drive not answering.

Said **once** per object, and again only after that object has answered. A
poll asks ten times a second, and the same abort ten times a second is one
fact told over and over.

**Set mode** reads 0x6061 back afterwards, so what the pane shows is the mode
the drive took rather than the one it was asked for -- a drive is free to
refuse a mode, or to take a moment over it. Without that, setting the mode
with polling switched off changed nothing visible and the target box went on
offering whatever the last mode allowed.

## Stopping

**Closing the pane is designed to stop the drive.** A demand sent over SDO does not stop
when the window showing it does: the drive holds the last controlword and the
last target it was given and goes on acting on them. So putting the pane away,
closing it for good, switching the plugin off or closing pycangui are all designed to halt the
drive first -- and so are *Stop all repeating traffic* and a [pause](https://github.com/davhodg/pycangui/blob/master/pycangui/help/pause.md), while
which nothing reads the drive; a pause does not set it moving again when it lifts -- and while it is running, a bar across the top of the pane says
so. So is a drive another plugin provides that is about to go out of reach on purpose -- an
EtherCAT ring about to be scanned, stopped or closed -- while it can still be written to. If it
was running and the bus goes instead, or the drive goes out of reach without warning, nothing
can be written, and the Event Log says that plainly rather than saying nothing. Do not rely on
any of this as an emergency stop: keep a way of stopping the machine that does
not depend on pycangui.

Stopping is a **halt** (controlword bit 8), not a zero target, and the
difference matters: halt means "come to a standstill" in every mode, whereas
zero is a *place*. Writing zero to the target position of a drive part way
through a move would not stop it -- it would send it to position zero, which
may be the longest move it has been asked for all day. A zero is written to
the target as well, but only in the speed and torque modes, where the target is
a rate and zero really is a stop.

Removing power on top of that is a tick box and is off by default.
It is not obviously the safer of the two: on a vertical axis it is the load
that decides, and whether a brake catches it is a fact about the machine rather
than about the tool.

## Units

Position and velocity are in the drive's own units. When a drive is picked
the pane reads **SI unit position** (0x60A8) and **SI unit velocity** (0x60A9),
and shows what they say beside every value, limit and target in those units:
*rpm*, *inc/s*, *0.1 °*. A drive without them -- older ones mostly -- shows
*position units* and *velocity units*, since nothing on the bus says which
they are. Torque is in per mille of rated torque. Only the two speed limits
are converted, into each other's unit (see [Limits](#limits)): turning values
into millimetres or amps needs the gearing and the motor rating, which are
the drive's business and not pycangui's.
